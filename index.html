Why URBAI

Individual convenience can create collective costs.

Navigation and shared-mobility systems optimize millions of individual choices. When those choices interact, they can concentrate traffic and emissions, alter access to urban opportunities, and reinforce spatial inequalities.

Research logic

From urban traces to responsible intervention.

URBAI connected empirical evidence, simulation, and algorithm design in a single workflow.

Observe

Use mobility and road-network data to characterize how people and services interact across urban space.

Simulate

Create controlled scenarios that vary adoption, demand, routing strategies, and relocation behavior.

Intervene

Test alternatives that balance personal utility with emissions, diversity, accessibility, and social well-being.

A central result

More adoption does not always mean more benefit.

Simulations across Florence, Milan, and Rome found that increasing adoption of navigation services can concentrate traffic and emissions onto fewer roads. At high traffic loads, emissions benefits weaken, disappear, or reverse after a city- and service-specific threshold.
